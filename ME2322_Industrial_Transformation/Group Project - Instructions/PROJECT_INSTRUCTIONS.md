## 1. What the project is

**Course component:** INL1, Part 1 — 1 ECTS total for INL1 (shared with the individual reflection in Part 2). Grading scale **Pass / Fail**.

**Deliverables:**
- A written report of **~1000–1500 words**, excluding appendix and references
- A **7-minute presentation** at your seminar
- Submission on Canvas by **24 November 2026, 12:00**

**What it actually is:** a research-design exercise, not an essay and not a build. You take a real transition happening in Swedish industry, pick one theory from the lectures, demarcate a narrow case, gather real evidence from the press and journals, and argue a specific claim. The marking interest is in the rigour of the design — the tightness of the question and the defensibility of your case boundary — not in the eloquence.

## 3. Choosing your topic

### Step 1 — Pick a theme

- Transitions towards more sustainable food production and consumption
- Transitions towards AI-based solutions in the energy sector
- Transition towards AI-based solutions in healthcare
- Adoption of large-language models
- A combination of the above, or similar

### Step 2 — Pick at least one theoretical concept (from L1–L4)

| Lecture | Concepts available |
|---|---|
| **L1** Industrial dynamics | Dominant design; general-purpose technologies (GPTs); the *Mastering the Dynamics of Innovation* model (Utterback 1996; Bresnahan 2024) |
| **L2** Socio-technical transitions | Technological Innovation Systems (TIS) and its functions (Bergek et al. 2008); Multi-Level Perspective — niches, regimes, landscape (Markard et al. 2012; Karakaya et al. 2018) |
| **L3** Diffusion of innovations | Adopter categories; change agents; the Wejnert (2002) integrated framework; diffusion mechanisms |
| **L4** Critical perspectives | Reflexivity; burden-shifting; critical perspectives on AI and sustainability transitions (Susur & Karakaya 2021; Bennich 2025; Lai & Karakaya 2024) |

### Step 3 — Demarcate a specific case ← *this is where marks are won and lost*

The course's own example: **"adoption of LLMs for knowledge creation in consulting companies."**

Note what makes that work: it names a technology (LLMs), a use (knowledge creation), and a bounded organisational setting (consulting firms). It is not "AI in business."

| ❌ Too broad | ✅ Defensible |
|---|---|
| "AI in healthcare" | "Adoption of AI triage tools in Swedish primary care, 2023–2026" |
| "Sustainable food" | "The diffusion of One Planet Plate among Swedish restaurant chains" |
| "LLMs at work" | "LLM adoption for knowledge creation in Swedish management consultancies" |
| "The energy transition" | "Flows of AI-based demand forecasting into Swedish district heating regimes" |

### Step 4 — Write your research question

One sentence, answerable with the evidence you can realistically gather in a week, and containing both the concept and the case. A useful shape: *"How does **[concept]** explain **[outcome]** in **[case]**?"*

Do not write a question you cannot answer from 5 newspaper articles and 5 journal papers.

## 4. Gathering evidence

### 4.1 Structured: Swedish press (mandatory)

- **4–5 newspaper articles** — interviews with industry experts, debate articles, related news
- Must come from **Svenska Dagbladet, Dagens Nyheter, and Dagens Industri** — **at least one from each**
- Access via **Mediearkivet (Retriever)** through the KTH Library
- For a long-running case, **Runestone** is usually the better KTH-accessible archive. Check the KTHB database list.
- Search on **Swedish keywords**, not English

### 4.2 Unstructured (supplementary)

Company websites, blogs, other news, annual reports, press releases, LinkedIn posts. Anything that fleshes out the case.

### 4.3 Academic journals (mandatory)

- **4–5 journal articles**
- Journals must be ranked **2 or higher** in the **Academic Journal Guide (CABS)** — i.e. 2, 3, 4 or 4\*
- Source: https://charteredabs.org/academic-journal-guide (**requires creating an account — do this first, it's a registration step that eats a day**)
- Choose them at the **intersection of your theoretical concept and your empirical case**

> Reality check: a 4\* journal rarely runs a paper on your exact Swedish niche case. Aim your search at the concept level. E.g. for a TIS analysis of district heating, you want papers on TIS function analysis in mature sectors, not on Swedish district heating specifically.

### 4.4 Guest lectures (mandatory)

- **Attend G1–G4** and be prepared to **ask questions**
- **At least one guest lecture must be referenced** in the report
- The guests are your practitioners — Anna Wahlberg (WWF), John Diklev (Flower), Tim Gisseman (Tandem), Patrik Ghirmai Juhl (King)
- Each lecture pairs the case with Emrah's ongoing research on it. That research framing is a strong signal for what he considers good analysis.

## 5. Report structure

Suggested structure from the course. Follow it — the marking follows it too.

### Introduction
Briefly motivate the theme and the problem you address, state the specific RQ, and state the theoretical angle you take.

### Methods
Summarise your strategy for choosing empirical data and theoretical approach, and **briefly explain why you chose them**. This is a short section but it is explicitly graded on reasoning.

### Findings
Summarise findings **in relation to the RQ**. Refer to newspaper articles, journal articles, and **at least one guest lecture**.

### Reflections on societal challenges / sustainability dilemmas
Critical reflection on how your findings relate to the most relevant societal challenges and/or sustainability issues.

### Appendix
Extra data, charts, tables, explanations that support the report. **Not counted in the word limit** — use it.

### Use of AI tools
Disclose whether you used any AI tools and, if so, how.

### References
Bibliography in **Harvard style**.

## 8. The seminar — the trap

Each team gets **up to 7 minutes to present** and **8 minutes of feedback**. Presentation format is free, but must let you properly explain what you did and learned.

**The trap:** group members are **distributed across different feedback groups**. You will **not** present alongside your teammates. **Every member must be ready to present the entire project alone on behalf of the team.**

Practical consequences:
- No "you take methods, I'll take findings"
- Everyone needs to know the RQ, the theory, the case, the evidence, and the argument
- Anyone could be presenting to a room of strangers with no teammates to bail them out

Beatriz is present throughout to support you and answer questions.

**If you cannot attend:** contact Emrah or Beatriz as soon as possible.

## 12. Grading

INL1 is **Pass/Fail**. There is no partial credit and no sliding scale — you either meet the brief or you don't.

The reliable failure modes:
- Theme stated instead of a case demarcated
- RQ that the evidence cannot answer
- Findings that describe instead of answering the RQ
- No guest lecture referenced
- Journal articles that aren't CABS 2+
- Newspaper articles missing a required source
- A member unable to present the project alone

Each of those is avoidable with a checklist. The project is not intellectually hard — it is administratively fiddly, and the marks are lost on the fiddly parts.

## 3. Slide structure for 7 minutes

Roughly one slide per minute, ~7 slides. Format is free — slides, whiteboard, handouts all work. Clarity beats polish.

| # | Slide | Time | Content |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Title + hook | 30s | Case, team, and one sentence on why this transition matters now |
| 2 | Research question | 30s | The RQ, stated verbatim. Nothing else on the slide. |
| 3 | Theory | 60s | The concept, one diagram (TIS functions diagram or MLP pyramid), and why it fits |
| 4 | Case + method | 60s | The demarcated case and how you gathered evidence — sources, date range, selection logic |
| 5 | Findings 1 | 75s | First analytical point with evidence |
| 6 | Findings 2 | 75s | Second point, ideally showing tension between sources |
| 7 | Reflections | 90s | The critical sustainability/societal point |
| 8 | Conclusion | 30s | Answer the RQ in one sentence |

**Cut mercilessly.** 7 minutes is shorter than it feels. Rehearse out loud with a timer — twice.

## 4. Questions to expect

Peer feedback is the format, so questions come from classmates who read your abstract, not your paper. Prepare crisp answers for:

**On the case**
- Why this case and not a broader one?
- Why this sector?
- Is it representative, or is it an outlier?

**On the theory**
- Why this concept over [the obvious alternative]?
- How does the theory actually explain what you found?
- What would a different lens have shown?

**On evidence**
- How did you choose those newspaper articles?
- Isn't press coverage biased toward novelty?
- Why no interviews / primary data?

**On the argument**
- Does your evidence actually support the RQ, or is it adjacent?
- What's the strongest counter-argument?
- What surprised you?

**Answering well:** acknowledge the limitation, then say what it would take to resolve it. "That's a real gap — with more time I'd have triangulated with company annual reports" beats deflection.

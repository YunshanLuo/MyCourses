**Aim:** acquire concepts, models and theories about industrial transformation and technological innovation, and use them to understand how major societal challenges affect industry and technology — and how those challenges can be handled. Builds on ME2321 *Engineering and the Global Challenges*.

**Learning outcomes**
1. Describe, explain and use concepts, models and theories to analyse industrial dynamics and technological innovation — including applied to Swedish industrial development.
2. Analyse how societal challenges affect industrial dynamics and innovation, and how innovation affects the societal response to those challenges.

## 4. Everything you must do

### 4.1 TEN1 — Oral exam (2 ECTS, A–F)

- **Individual** examination, but taken **three students at a time**.
- **Self-book a slot** on one of: **3, 4, 7, 8 or 9 December** (plus week 50 dates).
- **Format:**
  1. **Quiz** — each student answers a mix of multiple-choice and open-ended questions **on paper**, independently.
  2. **Reflection** — the examiner collects the papers and asks each student follow-up questions to reflect deeper, one after another.
- Questions cover **both theory and practice cases** from lectures and seminars.
- Further details given in lectures and on Canvas ahead of the exam.

### 4.2 INL1 Part 1 — Group work (Pass/Fail)

> 📁 **Full project pack:** [`Group Project - Instructions/`](Group%20Project%20-%20Instructions/README.md) — brief, report template, sourcing guide, seminar prep, 10-day team planner.

**Team:** 4–5 students. Self-assign to a seminar group slot (Groups A–D, Teams 1–8 each).

**Deliverables:**
- Short report, **1000–1500 words** excluding appendix and references
- **7-minute presentation** at your seminar
- **Submit by 12:00 on 24 November**

**Seminar schedule**

| Group | When |
|---|---|
| A | 24 Nov, 13:00–15:00 |
| B | 24 Nov, 15:00–17:00 |
| C | 25 Nov, 10:00–12:00 |
| D | 25 Nov, 13:00–15:00 |

~8 teams per group, so ~64 teams total.

**How to do the group work**

1. **Pick a theme:**
   - Transitions towards more sustainable food production and consumption
   - Transitions towards AI-based solutions in the energy sector
   - Transition towards AI-based solutions in healthcare
   - Adoption of large-language models
   - A combination of the above, or similar

2. **Choose at least one theoretical concept** from L1–L4, and **demarcate a specific case** within your theme (e.g. "adoption of LLMs for knowledge creation in consulting companies").

3. **Gather empirical data — structured:**
   - 4–5 newspaper articles: interviews with industry experts, debate articles or related news
   - From **Svenska Dagbladet, Dagens Nyheter and Dagens Industri** — **at least one from each**
   - Access via **Mediearkivet at KTHB**

4. **Gather empirical data — unstructured:** company websites, blogs, other news articles.

5. **Attend the guest lectures and ask questions** — incorporate those insights into the report. *At least one guest lecture must be referenced.*

6. **Find 4–5 journal articles** ranked **2 or higher** (2, 3, 4, 4\*) in the **Academic Journal Guide (CABS)** — https://charteredabs.org/academic-journal-guide (account required). Choose them at the intersection of your theoretical concept and empirical case.

**Report structure**

| Section | Content |
|---|---|
| Introduction | Motivate the theme and problem, state the specific RQ and the theoretical angle |
| Methods | How you chose empirical data and theoretical approach, and why |
| Findings | Summarise findings against your RQ; cite newspaper and journal articles and **at least one guest lecture** |
| Reflections on societal challenges / sustainability dilemmas | Critical reflection linking findings to relevant societal/sustainability issues |
| Appendix | Extra data, charts, tables, explanations |
| References | **Harvard style** |

**Plus:** disclose any AI tools used and how.

### 4.3 INL1 Part 2 — Individual reflection (Pass/Fail)

~**500 words**, due **8 January 2027**, covering your critical and reflexive learning process. Must include:

1. To what extent lectures and course literature supported your learning
2. What feedback you received in the seminars, and how seminars affected your learning
3. How the oral exam affected your learning process

> ⚠️ **Take notes during your seminar.** The peer feedback you receive is explicitly required material for this reflection two months later. This is the single easiest thing to lose points on.

**Seminar mechanics worth knowing:** each team presents for up to 7 minutes then receives 8 minutes of feedback. **Group members are distributed across different feedback groups** — so you will **not** present alongside your teammates, and **every member must be able to present the whole project alone.**

## 8. How to actually do well

1. **Theory is the currency.** The oral exam asks you to *apply* a concept from L1–L4 to a case. If you can't state Bergek's TIS functions or the MLP levels from memory, you can't answer.
2. **Take structured notes per paper**: author, year, core claim, and *how it applies to a case*. That note format is literally what the oral exam quizzes.
3. **The report is a research design exercise, not an essay.** Marks come from a tight RQ, a defensible case demarcation, and a theory–case intersection. Vague themes score badly.
4. **Get the newspaper spread right**: 4–5 articles, SvD + DN + DI, at least one from each.
5. **CABS 2+ journals** — set up the account early, it's a registration step that eats time.
6. **Every team member must be ready to present solo.** Don't split the report into "your part" and "my part."
7. **Write seminar feedback notes the same day.** The January reflection is graded on that material.
